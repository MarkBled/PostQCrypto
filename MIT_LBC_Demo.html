Matrix-Index Transmission Protocol (MIT-LBC)

Interactive Proof of Concept via Lattice-Based Cryptography

This interactive prototype demonstrates the asymmetric noise resilience of the MIT-LBC architecture. It shows how the isolated matrix M utilizes high-dimensional lattice rounding to recover the secret index from transmission noise (induced by fiber degradation or Eve's eavesdropping) without transmitting ciphertext.

Notice the threshold: within safe mathematical bounds, Bob's local matrix allows him to successfully decode the index while Eve faces an uncomputable chaotic space (NP-hard). However, if the noise amplitude shifts the vector beyond the strict lattice quantization bounds (e.g., higher error magnitudes), the protocol intentionally fails to prevent compromise, demonstrating the real-world physical limits of the system.
